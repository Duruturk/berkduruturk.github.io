About

Berk Durutürk is a geological engineer and a Ph.D. candidate at Aquatic Biogeochemistry Lab of Dr. Silvia Newell at the University of Michigan - Ann Arbor. His research mainly focuses on nitrogen transformations, harmful algal blooms (HABs), and hypoxic events in Lakes Erie and Superior.

His research interests also include paleolimnological reconstruction and paleoclimate proxies. Before joining the Newell Lab, he studied Late Pleistocene and Holocene paleoclimate records in lake sediments of the Eastern European Plain at St. Petersburg State University. Earlier, during his B.Sc. and M.Sc. in Türkiye, he worked on active faults, landslides and coastal carbonate deposits in Central Anatolia and along the Aegean and Mediterranean coasts.

Research Interests

  • Stable Isotopic Biogeochemical Techniques in Paleolimnology
  • Sediment Nitrogen Transformations and Biomarkers
  • Nutrient Dynamics at the Sediment-water Interfaces
  • Sediment Redox Ladder Sequence
  • Aquatic Biogeochemistry of the Great Lakes
